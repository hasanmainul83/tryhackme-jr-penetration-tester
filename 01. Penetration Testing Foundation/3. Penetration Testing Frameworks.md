Imagine you've just landed your first **penetration testing engagement**. The client wants you to test their web app, poke around their internal network, and see if employees can spot a phish.

You open your laptop—and then what?

Do you start with `nmap`? Try SQL injection on the login page? Send a phishing email to an employee?

**That's where these methodologies and references come in.** They help guide different parts of a penetration test, from planning and testing to understanding attacker behavior.

Here are six methodologies and references worth knowing in penetration testing.

- **OSSTMM** — Open Source Security Testing Methodology Manual
    
- **OWASP WSTG** — OWASP Web Security Testing Guide
    
- **NIST SP 800-115** — National Institute of Standards and Technology Special Publication 800-115
    
- **PTES** — Penetration Testing Execution Standard
    
- **ISSAF** — Information Systems Security Assessment Framework
    
- **MITRE ATT&CK** — MITRE Adversarial Tactics, Techniques, and Common Knowledge





Now, let's see what each one is actually good for.

- **OSSTMM** — The numbers guy. Scientific, metric-driven. You want hard data, this is it.
    
- **OWASP WSTG** — Your web app checklist. Every vuln, how to test it, so you don't miss anything.
    
- **NIST SP 800-115** — The gov playbook. Rigid process: plan, execute, report. Compliance-friendly.
    
- **PTES** — The practical guide. Scoping to reporting. This is how a real pentest actually flows.
    
- **ISSAF** — Old-school. Legacy. Know it exists, then stick to PTES or OWASP.
    
- **MITRE ATT&CK** — Not a pentest framework, it's a knowledge base. Real attacker behavior: tactics are the _goal_, techniques are the _how_. Use it for red teaming, threat hunting, and detection. If you don't know this, you're behind.


**Why they matter:** They give you a structure — how to think, how to test, how to report. Without one, you're just randomly poking. With one, you're a professional. They keep you from missing things, help you explain your findings, and connect your work to real-world threats. Pick the right tool for the job.





**For my path — The 3:**

- **PTES** — Scoping to reporting. The flow.
    
- **OWASP WSTG** — Web app checklist. Every vuln.
    
- **MITRE ATT&CK** — Red team brain. Tactic = goal, Technique = how.
    

I'd follow this.
